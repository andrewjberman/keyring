# /prune-qual — find the meaning, keep the foundation, rebuild the clean path

**This is not editing.** A document argues from inside its own premises; this skill interrogates
the premises. The deliverable is an understanding of what the thing is FOR — deep enough that the
question "what do we cut?" mostly answers itself. Sibling `/prune-quant` prunes what RUNS; this
prunes what is MEANT.

## The method — outside-in, meaning before sentences

**1. STAND OUTSIDE THE FRAME (exogenous stance).** Before reading closely, answer from the
system's vantage, not the author's: *What world-state does this exist to change? If the entire
artifact vanished tonight, what would actually be worse — for whom, by when, measured how?* If the
honest answer is "little, slowly, unclearly," the finding is about the thing's EXISTENCE, not its
wording — say so plainly. The document cannot ask this question about itself; that is the whole
reason this step exists.

**2. STATED PURPOSE vs REVEALED PURPOSE.** What the document says it is for, and what its
structure actually optimizes for (where the words are spent, what gets defended, what is
conspicuously absent). Divergence is the highest-value finding in this skill: a proposal that
SAYS "reliability" but SPENDS its pages on instrumentation is an alerting proposal wearing a
reliability costume — name the costume. The revealed purpose is what will actually get built.

**3. FOUNDATIONAL vs INCIDENTAL — the NEGATION TEST.** Find the 1-3 claims, decisions, or
mechanisms that are load-bearing: **negate each candidate and watch what happens.** Negate a
foundational element and the proposal collapses or becomes a *different* proposal. Negate an
incidental one and the proposal shrugs — it was derivable, replaceable, or ornament. Everything
in the artifact is now sorted by that test, not by how prominent the author made it. (Prominence
tracks author effort; foundation tracks consequence. They correlate poorly.)

**4. THE EXOGENOUS CHALLENGES — questions the document must survive from outside:**
- *Problem or symptom?* Is this aimed at the cause or at where the pain surfaced? (One layer up
  or down often dissolves the proposal entirely.)
- *Does this need to exist?* Does something already do this — a mechanism, a rule, a habit — such
  that the correct proposal is a pointer, an amendment, or nothing?
- *What does it commit us to?* Every mechanism must be maintained, verified, taught, and someday
  pruned; every rule taxes every future reader. Is the standing cost priced, or only the build?
- *What is it optimizing that nobody said out loud?* (Author convenience, blame protection,
  the appearance of rigor, a metric that stopped mattering.)
- *What would its strongest critic say the real point is?* Steelman the opposition; if the
  proposal absorbs the critique, that belongs in it — if it cannot, that belongs in the verdict.

**5. RECONSTRUCT THE CLEAN PATH.** Knowing the purpose (1-2) and the foundation (3): rebuild the
shortest honest argument from purpose to foundation to consequence. This reconstruction — not the
original's outline — is the standard. Everything in the original either sits on the path, earns a
one-line pointer off it, or goes. "Get there cleanly" means re-deriving the argument, never
sanding the prose of a bloated one.

**6. THE FINISHING CUT (last and least).** Only now the sentence-level pass: per-clause act test
(would the named reader DO anything differently without it?), the scar is not the rule (incident
history → pointers; the mechanism stays), one home per fact, and the named verbosity patterns
(throat-clearing, hedge-stacks, defensive completeness, importance-inflation). If steps 1-5 were
done honestly this step is mostly mechanical — which is the sign they were.

## Report format

```
# PRUNE-QUAL: <target>
MEANING:       what this is actually about, restated from OUTSIDE its frame, 2-3 sentences
STATED vs REVEALED: <purpose the doc claims> / <purpose its structure optimizes> / <divergence verdict>
FOUNDATIONAL:  the 1-3 load-bearing elements, each with its negation result
INCIDENTAL:    what shrugged under negation -- derivable / replaceable / ornament
CHALLENGES:    the exogenous questions it failed or must absorb, one line each
RECONSTRUCTION: the clean purpose->foundation->consequence path (the new skeleton)
FINISHING CUTS: the mechanical list (act-test fails, scars->pointers, verbosity patterns)
VERDICT:       proceed as reconstructed / amend foundation / should not exist as proposed
```

## Notes
- Report-first; the owner lands edits through their lane. A "should not exist" verdict routes to
  the owner and their exec — this skill can recommend killing a proposal, never execute it.
- Self-application: this file's own foundation is steps 1+3 (exogenous stance, negation test).
  Everything else here is reconstruction and finishing. Negate those two and this is /proofread.
