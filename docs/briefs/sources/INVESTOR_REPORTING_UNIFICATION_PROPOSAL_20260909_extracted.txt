[Kicker] 01  /  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
[Heading1] The problem and the solution
[Heading2] Problem | The client’s workflow is fragmented
Opal Capital’s advisory-firm clients need three closely related capabilities: up-to-date performance reporting for an account or client relationship; sensitivity analysis showing how the portfolio might respond to selected stress scenarios; and visibility into the Opal model portfolios represented in those holdings. Today, these jobs are distributed across separate systems. An advisor may obtain performance reports from Black Diamond or Orion, request stress analysis through YCharts, and consult SmartX or another model-management view to understand model allocations.
That fragmentation prevents a single, cohesive client-service workflow. The advisor must move between platforms to answer related questions about the same investor, while Opal must reconcile different providers, data shapes, access arrangements, and refresh timings. The product is therefore not simply missing three buttons: the underlying information must be organized consistently enough that those buttons refer to the same client, accounts, holdings, and reporting context.
Opal Invest began as an internal portfolio-analytics and investment-committee tool. It has since become client-facing and contains substantial client information, but its foundational experience still reflects Opal’s internal business-intelligence priorities. Continuing to force investor reporting into that legacy BI experience risks making both the product and its supporting pipelines harder to understand, maintain, and eventually simplify.
[Heading2] Solution | One advisor-first workspace on a common data foundation
Create a dedicated product, provisionally named Investor Reporting, inside Opal Invest. Preserve Advisor Intelligence as the BI product rather than stretching it into a general-purpose reporting page. Reuse the valuable foundation—multi-tenant controls, the firm-to-account hierarchy, established bronze/silver source feeds, and suitable interface components—but give Investor Reporting its own product experience and independently owned gold and platinum pipelines.
The new workspace will let an advisor search or navigate a book of business, inspect underlying securities and model allocations, and generate performance reports and sensitivity-analysis PDFs at the account or relationship level. Model-allocation views will also aggregate upward to advisor/book and firm level. Supervision and billing-status filters will be available from the top of the hierarchy and remain consistent throughout the workflow. Client-distributed artifacts must carry the disclosures and presentation standards reviewed by compliance.
[BodyText] WHY THIS BENEFITS THE CLIENT  The advisor gets a single place to answer “What does this client own, how has it performed, how might it behave under stress, and which Opal models are involved?” The investor receives coherent, client-ready outputs rather than a package assembled from disconnected platforms.
The near-term value is a better client-service workflow. The strategic value is a managed, consolidated data foundation that can progressively replace vendor dependence and later support agent-driven analysis over the same governed information.

[Kicker] 02  /  BACKGROUND AND CURRENT STATE
[Heading1] From internal utility to client foundation
[Heading2] Where the product began
The Opal Invest dashboard, identified in the conversation as opalinvest.dev, originated as an internal tool for Opal Capital’s portfolio analytics and investment committee. Its initial design naturally prioritized the questions Opal needed to answer about assets, partners, cohorts, and investment operations. The current product should be understood in that historical context: its choices were made for the users and purposes known at the time, rather than as a fully specified client-reporting platform.
The dashboard has since expanded into a client-facing product with a substantial amount of client information. Opal now wants it to become a foundational tool for its advisory-firm clients. That change introduces a different organizing principle. Internal BI often begins with a broad population and investigates patterns; an advisor preparing for a client interaction often begins with a named relationship or account and needs actionable, exportable information about that particular investor.
[Heading2] What is already valuable
The user explicitly rejected the idea that this is a massive pivot. Much of the information is presented correctly, many pipelines are set up correctly, and the existing hierarchy is strategically useful. Advisor Intelligence already provides a top-down route from firm through advisor and relationship to account. Model Portfolios already provides model-allocation summaries. Positioning already presents the characteristics and constituent holdings of the models. The underlying AWS serverless pattern and the bronze/silver source foundation are also established.
The proposal therefore distinguishes reuse from entanglement. Working source ingestion, identity controls, hierarchy concepts, validated mapping logic, and suitable components can be carried forward. A historical page’s particular composition of gold data, serving files, and schedules should not automatically become a permanent dependency of the new product. Preservation of useful infrastructure is compatible with a fresh product surface.
[Heading2] Where the high-leverage choices sit
The challenge lies in a small set of foundational forks with consequences across both product and engineering: whether to extend Advisor Intelligence or split the experience; where to define the common partner data shape; how to support today’s uneven vendor access while preparing for custodian-first operation; how to preserve model attribution; and how to establish independent paths and a consistent daily release without multiplying technical debt.
The user’s concern about legacy modification is central. A team can spend substantial effort bending an existing product into a new purpose, even when a direct design of the desired product would be clearer. The recommended approach is to specify the desired client experience first, identify exactly which established foundations support it, and then integrate deliberately. This does not justify ignoring migration; it makes migration an explicit engineering workstream rather than allowing inherited dependencies to choose the product design.
[Heading2] The resulting program
Investor Reporting is the immediate delivery vehicle. Data unification is the enabling program. Migration governance is what keeps the new work from becoming another legacy tangle. Together, these three elements explain why the proposal covers product experience, data contracts, source adapters, ownership boundaries, scheduling, compliance-reviewed outputs, and retirement criteria rather than only a set of dashboard enhancements.

[Heading1] Client reporting, stress analysis, and models
The three capabilities belong together because they answer related questions about the same investor. They should share account identity, relationship membership, holdings context, filters, and data provenance. They do not, however, have identical scope: performance reporting and sensitivity analysis are required at account and relationship level; holdings and model-allocation visibility must also work at advisor/book and firm level.

[Heading2] Capability 1 | Up-to-date performance reporting
The advisor must be able to search for a client, identify the relevant account or relationship, and produce a report for that selected scope. At relationship level, the scope is the specified group of accounts rather than an unrelated top-level cohort. The existing external workflow generally involves Black Diamond or Orion, an account or relationship lookup, and a PDF export containing familiar investor-reporting information: the reporting period, changes in value, and the account’s ups and downs.
The proposed product must provide that basic client-facing job cleanly before broadening into more elaborate analysis. The minimum experience is scope selection, period selection, a readable preview, and a client-ready PDF. Recommended report fields include the selected client and account roster, beginning and ending values, relevant flows, investment results under the approved methodology, source/as-of dates, and disclosures. Benchmark comparisons and further analytical detail should be included only where the product and investment owners approve the requirement and data.
[Heading2] Define calculation ownership before replacing a vendor
The conversation established the required reporting outcome, but did not select a performance methodology. The implementation must explicitly decide how returns are defined, which periods are supported, how cash flows and fees are treated, how account histories are combined, and how corrections are handled. Time-weighted versus money-weighted presentation, gross versus net reporting, benchmark selection, and the treatment of incomplete history remain decisions—not assumptions that a developer should make while wiring a button.
Initially, a report may rely on normalized, authorized vendor results if that is the approved transitional method. It must then preserve the vendor, methodology basis, and data lineage rather than implying the result was independently calculated by Opal. Direct-custodian replacement requires an approved internal calculation path or another accepted source for any result the departing platform supplied. A current holdings snapshot alone is not an adequate specification for historical performance reporting.
[Heading2] Client-ready output is part of the product
PDF artifacts are a required deliverable, not a later cosmetic enhancement. Recommended controls include versioned templates, the scope and period used, the data snapshot and calculation version, the applicable disclosure version, and an export audit record. Compliance must approve the necessary disclosures and the review process; this proposal does not invent legal wording or assert that an existing vendor’s disclosures can simply be copied.
[BodyText] ACCEPTANCE TEST  An authorized advisor can find a relationship, verify the included accounts, review an approved performance presentation, and export a PDF that reproduces the same scope and values with the correct disclosures.
[Kicker] 04  /  CAPABILITIES — CONTINUED
[Heading1] Sensitivity analysis and model visibility
[Heading2] Capability 2 | Portfolio sensitivity analysis
Clients regularly ask how their portfolio might behave in a downturn. The current workflow described by the user uses a company such as YCharts. The new workspace should make preset stress scenarios available at both account and relationship level, show the resulting analysis in context, and generate an exportable PDF. Scenarios discussed were 2008, an unspecified “2016 issue,” and the August 2024 yen carry-trade episode. The exact scenario dates and definitions have not been selected; the 2016 reference must remain unresolved until clarified.
The user’s working explanation was that a provider may take historical shocks or volatility in major indexes and apply portfolio-holding betas to estimate responses, with duration used for fixed income. Preserve this as a hypothesis about the existing service, not a verified description of YCharts’ methodology and not an approved Opal model. Historical replay, factor-based estimation, and a prescribed hypothetical shock are different candidate analytical approaches. The final choice must be documented and validated before client distribution.
Recommended scenario metadata includes a scenario identifier, name, event window, reference series or shocks, estimation method, portfolio snapshot, eligible holdings, coverage limitations, and version. The investment owner must decide how unsupported securities, missing beta or duration, cash, funds, model look-through, interest-rate changes, and any relevant credit or currency effects are handled. Do not silently assign a zero response to missing data. The output should distinguish a modeled result from realized performance or a prediction.
The PDF should name the selected scenario, portfolio scope, data date, method, and limitations, alongside the approved disclosures. Scenario naming, calibration, data rights, and calculation ownership are implementation gates. A familiar label such as “2008” is not enough to make results comparable across implementations.
[Heading2] Capability 3 | See the Opal models, not only the securities
When viewing a client account, an advisor needs to see which Opal model portfolios account for the assets—not merely a flat list of underlying securities. The same model-allocation view must aggregate to the relationship, advisor/book, and firm. This is the principal reason to preserve the aggregation spine: model exposure should compose and decompose consistently across all four levels, while performance and stress actions remain at the two client-specific levels.
Model attribution must distinguish actual client allocations from a model’s reference composition. SmartX, Eclipse, or Black Diamond may hold the relevant assignments under today’s arrangements. The account-to-model mapping, effective dates, allocation values, and treatment of residual or unassigned assets therefore require explicit gold contracts. Where multiple model allocations exist within an account, those allocations must reconcile to the appropriate account population without double-counting the same underlying asset.
Unknown model attribution should remain visible as an exception rather than disappearing from an “all assets” total or being guessed from matching securities. Product owners must decide how to display non-model holdings, cash, and overlapping or partial model assignments. These decisions affect every roll-up and are a prerequisite to trustworthy model summaries.
[BodyText] KEEP THE PAGE BOUNDARY  Investor Reporting explains the models a client holds. Positioning explains a model’s constituent holdings and characteristics—duration, beta, sector exposures, and similar attributes. Link to that existing reference where useful; do not duplicate its full purpose.

[Kicker] 06  /  THE FOUNDATIONAL PRODUCT STRATEGY FORK
[Heading1] Reuse the foundation. Separate the job.
[Heading2] The alternatives considered
The first alternative is to extend Advisor Intelligence: keep the existing top-down page and add performance exports, sensitivity analysis, and model views where the user reaches a client or account. This has an obvious attraction because the hierarchy and some information already exist. It was the initial working idea in the conversation. The risk is that product structure and data dependencies continue to be dictated by a BI experience built for a different primary user and purpose.
The second alternative is to design Investor Reporting directly around the desired client workflow, then reuse the best foundations from Advisor Intelligence. The user identified this as the primary product fork and endorsed the clean split. A new page or product should include all three required capabilities and the full firm-to-account hierarchy, while Advisor Intelligence retains its original BI role. The provisional name is Investor Reporting; final naming has not been settled.

[Heading2] Recommended and endorsed direction
Proceed with the dedicated product. “Separate” means independent product intent and downstream data ownership, not a second company-wide infrastructure stack or a second raw ingestion estate. Reuse the multi-tenant foundation and aggregation spine. Start from the existing bronze/silver assets where appropriate. Reuse proven code only after its assumptions and dependencies are understood. Build the new gold and platinum channels to serve the new contract directly.
This approach addresses the user’s central development concern: it may be more complex to repeatedly twist a legacy product into the intended outcome than to design that outcome explicitly and integrate the appropriate foundations. The objective is not novelty for its own sake. It is a simpler correspondence among the user’s job, the page structure, the canonical data, and the pipeline that produces it.
[Heading2] What must not happen during implementation
Do not create a new front-end tab while leaving it dependent on old Model Portfolios or Advisor Intelligence gold files. Do not copy those pipelines wholesale without removing hidden assumptions and documenting ownership. Do not make each partner a separate product because its vendor sends a different shape. Do not use the new build as an excuse to replace working bronze/silver ingestion or redesign Positioning unnecessarily.
[BodyText] DECISION D01  Create a distinct Investor Reporting workspace within Opal Invest, using common source and platform foundations but independently governed Investor Reporting gold, platinum, schedules, and publication paths.
Before build approval, the team should be able to draw the new dependency boundary and demonstrate which changes to legacy BI or model-summary pipelines would no longer affect the new reporting product. That is the practical test of whether the split is real.
[Kicker] 07  /  PROPOSED INVESTOR REPORTING EXPERIENCE
[Heading1] A single workflow from book to client output
[Heading2] Entry, search, and persistent context
The advisor enters an authorized firm context and can inspect the firm’s book, navigate to an advisor/book, search for a relationship or account, and drill into the chosen scope. Firm, advisor/book, relationship/client/household, and account are views of the same hierarchy rather than disconnected screens with independent populations. A persistent context should show the active firm, selected scope, cohort filters, valuation/as-of information, and the current release or data-status indicator.
Search is as important as top-down navigation. The client-facing job often begins with a name or account rather than an exploratory firm-level analysis. The user should therefore be able to go directly to a permitted relationship or account without traversing each level, while still seeing where the selected entity sits in the hierarchy. Search results must respect the same tenant and book permissions as the rest of the product.

[Heading2] Representative advisor journey
An advisor opens the firm view, chooses supervised and billable assets, and selects a book. They search for a client relationship, confirm the included accounts, and inspect both the securities and model allocations. They select a reporting period, review the performance report, and export the approved PDF. From that same relationship context they select an approved stress scenario, inspect coverage and limitations, and export the sensitivity-analysis artifact. At no point should the product silently change the client population or filter state.
The advisor can then open one account to investigate a component of the relationship and return to the parent scope without losing the chosen context. At firm or book level, they can inspect aggregate holdings and model allocations, but the performance and stress actions are not required. Product design should avoid implying that unsupported top-level analyses exist merely because the lower-level buttons are present elsewhere.
[Heading2] Read functions first; tightly scoped editing later
The user described the first service layer as reporting and read functions, with possibly slight editing. This proposal does not infer trading, account maintenance, portfolio implementation, or unrestricted data mutation. Any limited edit—such as a report label or an approved grouping correction—requires a separately specified owner, validation rule, audit trail, and relationship to source-of-record data before it is added.

[Kicker] 08  /  ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES AND NON-NEGOTIABLES
[Heading1] Contracts before paths; independence by design
[Heading2] 1. Unification is the product philosophy
Opal intends to act as a unifier, consolidator, and managed service provider. Disparate source information should be compiled into one managed data lake and a common format so advisors can obtain the information and analysis they need in one place. The user explicitly withdrew “sovereignty” as the framing. Do not substitute a different positioning, deployment model, or data-ownership proposition for the managed-service objective stated here.
[Heading2] 2. Preserve source evidence; normalize at the correct boundary
Bronze preserves the original feed. Silver produces clean, typed, deduplicated Parquet while retaining every source column. Gold is where source-specific shapes converge into the common business and analytical contract. Platinum is the JSON delivery layer for the portal. This division must be visible in code, manifests, storage paths, tests, and responsibilities; merely naming four folders after metals does not establish the architecture.
[Heading2] 3. Same gold contract across partners; separate channels across products
Within Investor Reporting, every partner must produce the same canonical datasets and field meanings regardless of its custodian or vendor. Across products, Investor Reporting must own its gold and platinum outputs rather than consuming Advisor Intelligence or Model Portfolios gold artifacts. These are complementary rules: cross-partner standardization does not require cross-product coupling. Shared libraries and versioned schemas can prevent inconsistent definitions without creating hidden runtime dependencies.
[Heading2] 4. Reuse the existing source foundation deliberately
The bucket referred to as “Opal Data assets” already contains bronze and silver data for all firms across custodians, data vendors, and model providers. The user considers that bucket and those pipelines broadly sound. Treat it as the initial source root. Audit coverage for the new capabilities and make targeted corrections where needed, but do not frame the program as a wholesale ingestion rebuild. The expected major effort is in gold normalization, business logic, and analytics.
[Heading2] 5. Carry three cross-cutting foundations into the new product
Multi-tenancy, the firm-to-advisor-to-relationship-to-account spine, and supervision/billing cohort slicing are the three implementation foundations explicitly prioritized by the user. [Kicker] 09  /  TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE AND GOLD CONTRACTS
[Heading1] The bronze–silver–gold–platinum contract
[Heading2] Bronze | Raw, daily, and unchanged
Bronze is the original daily feed in whatever form the source supplies: CSV, XLSX, JSON, or another format. It is unaltered source evidence. Do not rename away source meaning, remove fields, filter the content to fit a page, or overwrite the evidence of what was received. Recommended operational metadata includes tenant, provider, source dataset or endpoint, acquisition timestamp, source as-of date when available, object checksum, and ingestion/run identifier. Metadata should accompany the raw payload without changing that payload.
[Heading2] Silver | Typed, clean Parquet with all columns retained
Silver converts the received data into typed Parquet, applies deliberate deduplication, and cleans the representation. The user emphasized twice that no columns are to be redacted or removed. A field that is irrelevant to the current dashboard can still be valuable for later analysis or agent services. Preserve it. Type mappings and deduplication rules should be explicit per source and dataset, with validation results recorded rather than silently discarding problematic input.
Deduplication must respect the source grain and correction behavior. Repeated transactions, holdings snapshots from different dates, and corrected versions are not automatically duplicate business events. Recommended handling is to preserve the raw evidence, identify the accepted silver record deterministically, and retain or quarantine exceptions for review. Schema changes and unparseable values should generate visible issues; cleaning is not permission to lose source information.
[Heading2] Gold | Canonical business meaning and analytics in Parquet
Gold is the point of consolidation and shaping. Each partner’s source-shaped silver inputs must map to the same Investor Reporting output structure, identity rules, field meanings, hierarchy, and analytical conventions. Gold is where business logic and analytics belong: account and relationship linkage, holdings normalization, model attribution, cohorts, valuations, and approved reporting or stress calculations. Keep these analytical outputs in Parquet rather than making the portal JSON the only surviving representation.
Recommended organization separates canonical facts and dimensions from derived analytical results within the Investor Reporting gold namespace. Both remain gold; this is an implementation subdivision, not a fifth medallion tier. Their schemas, transformation versions, and source lineage should be recorded so that an agent can distinguish an observed position, an assigned model allocation, and a calculated result.
[Heading2] Platinum | JSON serving outputs for the portal
Platinum consists of JSONs in the portal bucket, prepared for the dashboard’s serving architecture. The existing pattern is AWS serverless, with Lambdas publishing or serving JSON for advisor views. Investor Reporting should retain that pattern while owning its namespace and view contracts. Platinum should shape gold into searchable lists, summary views, drill-down data, and analysis responses—not introduce competing business calculations that exist nowhere in gold.
PDF exports are separate, governed artifacts produced from the same approved analytical context. Their existence does not change the user’s requirement that platinum dashboard payloads be JSON. The exact export-storage location, retention policy, and generation mechanism remain implementation decisions and should be recorded separately from the platinum data contract.
[BodyText] LAYER FLOW  Daily source feeds → preserved bronze → full-column typed silver Parquet → canonical Investor Reporting gold Parquet → Investor Reporting platinum JSON → advisor views and governed export generation.

Recommended measures are client-workflow completion, system switching avoided, account/model/cohort coverage, reconciliation exceptions, source freshness, successful morning publications, export success, unresolved tenant-access defects, and redundant pipeline cost. Set targets after baselines and source constraints are known. The proposal does not claim that vendor replacement, a particular saving, or a precise completion date is guaranteed by the architectural choice.

[Heading2] Explicit trade-offs to accept
Independent product channels may temporarily cost more during parallel validation, but they create a controlled exit from legacy coupling. Vendor-specific adapters add near-term complexity, but avoid blocking the product until every custodian is accessible. Retaining all source columns increases governance and storage responsibilities, but preserves evidence and future analytical options. A fixed release cadence improves predictability, but must accommodate visible differences in source freshness.
No current codebase audit, source entitlement review, financial-methodology approval, compliance sign-off, or real bucket inventory was performed as part of this document. Those are implementation deliverables, not missing facts to be filled in by inference. The proposal provides the structure in which they can be completed without losing the user’s intended product or architecture.
[Kicker] 17  /  CONCLUSION AND EXECUTION HANDOFF
[Heading1] The program to approve and carry forward
[Heading2] The complete direction
Opal Invest should evolve from an internally oriented analytics tool into a foundational managed service for advisory firms. The immediate client benefit is a unified Investor Reporting workspace that brings performance reporting, sensitivity analysis, and Opal model-allocation visibility together around the same client and account context. The advisor is the primary user; client-ready, compliance-reviewed PDF outputs are part of the required product, not an optional finishing step.
The clean product split is the key decision. Advisor Intelligence stays focused on BI. Model Portfolios remains a global and firm-level summary product. Positioning remains the model-characteristics reference. Investor Reporting owns the client workflow, inherits the multi-tenant and hierarchy foundations, and keeps supervision and billing eligibility independently sliceable from the firm level downward.
The medallion architecture supports that split: raw daily bronze, full-column typed and deduplicated silver Parquet, normalized analytical gold Parquet, and platinum JSON for the portal. All partners converge to the same gold contract. Investor Reporting’s gold and platinum channels are independent of the legacy gold pipelines for Advisor Intelligence and Model Portfolios, even when they begin with the same approved source bucket.
The transition begins with the access Opal actually has. 49 Financial provides the custodian-first reference pattern; Inflection and Cottonwood initially require their existing vendor-shaped inputs. Direct custody access, retained history, and authoritative model assignments enable later replacement. A path manifest, consistent schedules, dependency-aware preparation, a governed 9:00 a.m. Eastern release, rollback, and explicit retirement criteria prevent the new product from becoming another source of debt.
